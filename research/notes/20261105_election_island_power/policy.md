## 1. Federal mechanisms WITHOUT a presidential signature

### 1a. Veto math (the constraint)
- Any new statute (GRID Act, moratorium, Ratepayer Protection Act variants) needs Trump's signature or 2/3 in both chambers. 54 D senators is far from 67; House 228 is far from 290. IAPP (2026-11-04): Democrats "will still need to work with President Trump or secure a veto-proof majority to pass any legislation" (paraphrase). https://iapp.org/news/a/democratic-midterm-wins-set-stage-for-tougher-tech-and-ai-scrutiny
- Fundstrat's Tom Lee (pre-results note, via Yahoo Finance 2026-11-04) "expects a Trump veto to block anti-AI or anti-data center bills" (paraphrase). https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/article/spacex-tesla-stocks-sink-as-analyst-warns-democrats-victory-will-create-major-hurdles-for-ai-build-out-135511616.html

### 1b. Congressional Review Act — CONFIRMED: needs signature
- CRS IF12708: a CRA joint resolution of disapproval "requires passage in both chambers of Congress and signature by the President to become law." When the rule is the sitting President's, he "can be expected to veto it," and overriding "requires a two-thirds majority of each house of Congress," creating "a de facto supermajority requirement for a CRA joint resolution of disapproval to be enacted in most cases." Lookback: rules submitted "less than 60 days of session in the Senate or less than 60 legislative days in the House of Representatives" before sine die adjournment get a fresh review window in the next session (treated as received on 15th legislative/session day). https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF12708.html
- **Implication (INFERENCE):** EPA rules finalized in late 2026 (e.g., minor-NSR public-participation rule if finalized; any "mobile source turbine" rule) will fall into the 2027 lookback window and Democrats can force floor votes (CRA resolutions are privileged in the Senate, need only 51 votes, no filibuster), but enactment requires Trump's signature. Value is political/record-vote, not legal. Reuters (2026-11-04) reported renewables rallied partly on "possible use of the Congressional Review Act to overturn regulations" — markets may be overestimating CRA leverage. https://finance.yahoo.com/energy/articles/ai-data-center-stocks-dip-113427491.html

### 1c. Appropriations riders — the real lever, but shared with the President
- **FY2027 timing:** Continuing resolution P.L. 119-103 funds government through **December 11, 2026** (House passed CR 370-48, Senate 90-6, in July 2026; signed). Without action, "a funding gap could begin Dec. 12." Sources: CRA GovAffairs blog 2026-09-03, https://cra.org/govaffairs/blog/2026/09/fy2027-sept-update/ ; Legis1 2026-10-22, https://legis1.com/news/fy2027-interior-appropriations-bill-rejects-18
- **Interior-Environment (EPA) bill:** H.R. 9171 (House Appropriations reported June 5, 2026) totals $42.67B; would cut EPA by $1.78B (20%) vs the President's requested 52% cut. No Senate action reported; no data-center or air-permitting language reported in the bill as of Oct 22. https://legis1.com/news/fy2027-interior-appropriations-bill-rejects-18
- **Mechanics (INFERENCE from standard practice):** Riders of the form "None of the funds made available by this Act may be used to finalize/implement [rule]" can be inserted in the Interior-Environment bill (EPA air permitting: minor-NSR public participation, turbine NSPS/mobile-source rule, "begin actual construction" NSR rule) and the Energy-Water bill (DOE/FERC: e.g., DOE Sec. 202(c) orders, DOE large-load guidance). A rider still must survive conference and signature, but a President vetoing an omnibus over an EPA rider is costly — this is the classic leverage point. Timing: either the Dec 11 lame-duck deal (current Republican Congress still in control — so limited Democratic leverage) or, more likely, a further CR into early 2027 when Democratic appropriators (new chairs) write the bills. **Open question:** whether the lame-duck Republican majority pushes a full-year CR to deny Democrats FY2027 bill-writing.

## 2. EPA status (2026)

### 2a. Minor-NSR public participation rule — PROPOSED, comment closed, NOT finalized (as found)
- Announced 2026-07-01 (EPA release): proposes to "remove the federal minimum requirements for public participation in state and local minor New Source Review (NSR) permitting" (paraphrase); Zeldin: "The state and local authorities closest to the issues and the public should be making the decisions"; "cutting unnecessary and burdensome red tape, unleashing American energy". 45-day comment period from FR publication. Release does not mention data centers. https://epa.gov/newsreleases/epa-proposes-streamline-state-and-local-permitting-process-minor-sources
- Federal Register: FR Doc. 2026-13667, published **2026-07-07** ("Minor New Source Review Program Air Permitting: Public Participation Requirements for State Implementation Plans"). Comment period therefore closed ~2026-08-21 (INFERENCE from 45 days; coalition comments filed 2026-08-21). Pre-publication PDF: https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2026-07/pre-publication-rule-minor-new-source-review-program-air-permitting-public-participation-requirements-for-state-implementation-plans.pdf
- Scope: Would not change existing state SIPs automatically; states keep current requirements "unless they revise them and EPA approves." PSD, nonattainment NSR, Title V unaffected. (Parr Brown 2026-07-07, https://parrbrown.com/environmental-law-update-epa-proposes-changes-to-public-participation-requirements-for-minor-nsr-programs/)
- Opposition: SELC-led coalition comments 2026-08-21: EPA would "eradicate minimum federal public participation requirements"; Earthjustice's David Baron: "disgraceful attempt to allow secret approvals". https://www.selc.org/press-release/coalition-urges-epa-to-keep-public-participation-requirements-for-air-permits/ ; EPN (Mike Koerber): "By eliminating the long-standing federal requirement for public notice, this proposal continues efforts by the current administration to turn its back on the fundamental EPA principle of transparency." https://www.environmentalprotectionnetwork.org/?p=39901 ; Axios Atlanta 2026-08-12 (Georgia data center diesel generators) https://www.axios.com/local/atlanta/2026/08/12/georgia-data-centers-diesel-generators-public-comment-pollution
- **Final rule:** No final rule or OMB-review entry found in searches as of 2026-11-05. **Litigation:** none (nothing to challenge until final). If finalized in Dec 2026–Jan 2027, it lands squarely in the CRA lookback window (see 1b) and is a prime rider target.

### 2b. Turbine NSPS (40 CFR 60 Subpart KKKKa) — FINAL
- Signed 2026-01-09; published **91 FR 1910, 2026-01-15** (FR Doc. 2026-00677). Applies to turbines that commenced construction/modification/reconstruction after **2024-12-13**. https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/15/2026-00677/new-source-performance-standards-review-for-stationary-combustion-turbines-and-stationary-gas
- NOx limits (new, natural gas, >70% load), per EPA fact sheet: Large (>850 MMBtu/h), ≥38% efficiency, high utilization (12-mo CF >45%) → **combustion controls + SCR, 5 ppm**; Large low-utilization → 25 ppm; Large <38% eff → 9 ppm; Medium (>50–850 MMBtu/h) high-util → 15 ppm, low-util → 25 ppm; Small (≤50) → 25 ppm. **Temporary turbines (≤850 MMBtu/h, ≤24 months at a location) → 25 ppm, manufacturer-certified, no annual testing.** A temporary turbine kept >24 months "is not treated as temporary for any part of its operation"; cannot swap temporary for temporary to extend. https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2026-01/final-rule_fact-sheet-nsps-stationary-combustion-turbines_0.pdf
- Data-center relevance: Clark Hill (2026-05-27): the rule "significantly reduces permit obligations for certain turbines used in data center applications"; low-use turbines exempted from Title V if not otherwise major. https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/epa-turbine-rules-air-permitting-data-centers/ ; ALL4 (2026-01-29): if temporary turbine stays longer, "U.S. EPA will retroactively apply the requirements of the rule as if the unit is now permanent"; EPA's general temporary-source definition "requires the unit to be on-site for no longer than 12 months" (conflict with 24-month NSPS subcategory). https://www.all4inc.com/4-the-record-articles/data-centers-2026-look-ahead/
- **Litigation on NSPS itself:** none found in searches (petitions for review would be in D.C. Circuit; not confirmed). **Related litigation:** *NAACP et al. v. X.AI Corp.*, No. 3:26-cv-74 (N.D. Miss., filed ~2026-04-14), CAA citizen suit over ~50 "temporary" turbines at xAI's Mississippi facility; DOJ filed brief 2026-06-15 arguing it can intervene/preempt (Greenberg Traurig 2026-06-23, https://www.gtlaw.com/ar/insights/2026/6/data-centers-and-air-quality-recent-changes-and-important-considerations-for-developers).

### 2c. "Mobile source" turbine treatment — NOT yet a rule
- The Jan 2026 NSPS contains a *conditional* exclusion: turbines leave the "stationary combustion turbine" definition only if they meet the nonroad-engine definition AND are certified to Title II standards — which "takes effect only when EPA adopts nonroad standards and certification requirements for portable combustion turbines, which requires a separate rulemaking" (Clark Hill paraphrase; 91 FR 1926 says turbines may be "a kind of internal combustion engine"). https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/epa-turbine-rules-air-permitting-data-centers/
- EPA air chief Aaron Szabo confirmed EPA "is considering a policy that would classify large on-site generators at data centers as nonroad or mobile sources instead of stationary sources" (Ballard Spahr/Law360 2026-05-15, paraphrase; title: "'Mobile' Sources For On-Site Generation May Be A Risky Bet"). https://www.ballardspahr.com/insights/alerts-and-articles/2026/05/mobile-sources-for-on-site-generation-may-be-a-risky-bet ; Frantz Ward 2026-05-19: "certain portable or truck-mounted units could be classified as 'mobile sources' under the Clean Air Act"; "uncertainty remains as to the scope and timing of these changes." https://www.frantzward.com/epa-moving-to-regulate-data-center-turbines-as-mobile-sources/
- **Status: no proposed rule found as of 2026-11-05.** If proposed/finalized in 2027, it would be the single most consequential federal action for BTM gas (removes NSR entirely for qualifying units) and the most exposed to a Democratic appropriations rider / oversight.

### 2d. NSR "begin actual construction" rule — PROPOSED (May 2026)
- Would allow non-emitting construction (foundations, shells, utility connections) before NSR permit "at their own risk"; comment closed 2026-06-29. Final not found. (Greenberg Traurig 2026-06-23.)

### 2e. RICE (reciprocating engines) — guidance only; prime power = major-source exposure
- EPA FAQ clarification **2025-05-01** (NESHAP Subpart ZZZZ): emergency engines "can operate for up to 50 hours per year in non-emergency conditions" to supply the grid under a financial arrangement (Duke PowerShare question). Zeldin: "This is the first, and certainly not the last step". https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/epa-issues-clarification-help-power-data-centers-ensure-us-ai-capital-world ; Columbia Sabin Center summary https://climate.law.columbia.edu/content/epa-guidance-allows-non-emergency-use-reciprocating-internal-combustion-engines-supply-data
- No 2026 EPA rule or guidance found that lets data-center RICE run as *prime/continuous* power under emergency-engine rules. ALL4 (2026-01-29): "50 hours per year limit on non-emergency is still in effect"; EPA's five financial-arrangement criteria still apply; whether prime and backup emissions can be treated as separate sources is an open question with no test given. Virginia presumptive BACT: 6.0 g/hp-hr NOx emergency vs **0.6 g/hp-hr non-emergency**; Illinois requires Tier 4 for new DC emergency gensets from 2026-01-08 (synthetic minor/CAAPP applications from 2026-12-01). https://www.all4inc.com/4-the-record-articles/data-centers-2026-look-ahead/
- **Answer to "do prime-power engines need major-source permits sooner than turbines?" (INFERENCE from the above):** Yes. Non-emergency RICE lose the emergency-engine carve-out (NSPS IIII/JJJJ non-emergency limits, NESHAP ZZZZ non-emergency requirements, state non-emergency BACT ~10x tighter), have no "temporary" subcategory analogous to KKKKa's 24-month turbine class, and no mobile-source pathway under consideration. A reciprocating-engine fleet sized for prime power at a 100+ MW campus will exceed 25–100 tpy NOx thresholds quickly (even with SCR) → major NSR/Title V in nonattainment areas, whereas turbines can use the temporary subcategory (25 ppm, 24 months) and low-use Title V exemption. Hence RICE is viable mainly for backup/limited-hours or in attainment areas with synthetic-minor caps.

### 2f. Texas nonattainment reconsideration (new, 2026-09-29)
- EPA announced it is reconsidering the DFW/HGB/San Antonio classification determinations and the 2022 disapproval of Texas's §179B (international emissions) demonstration; "plans to propose reconsideration actions by the end of this year and finalize all actions next year." No FR document yet; all current thresholds remain enforceable. (iSi Environmental 2026-09-30, https://isienvironmental.com/blog/texas-ozone-nonattainment-air-permitting-requirements/). **INFERENCE:** a successful reconsideration would relax Texas BTM gas permitting dramatically (from 25 tpy to 50/100 tpy) — a 2027 Democratic oversight / rider target.

## 5. Hyperscaler / industry response

### 5a. Ratepayer Protection Pledge
- **Original (2026-03-04, White House):** signatories **Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, Oracle, xAI**. Trump: "Big Tech companies are committing to fully cover the cost of increased electricity production required for AI data centers."; Sacks: companies will "cover the costs of all new power generation required for their data centers". Five commitments: (1) build/bring/buy new power supply; (2) pay for all new power delivery upgrades; (3) pay negotiated rates "whether or not they actually use the electricity"; (4) local hiring/workforce; (5) coordinate with grid operators incl. making backup generation available during shortages. https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/03/president-trump-secures-historic-commitment-to-keep-electricity-costs-down-amid-data-center-boom
- **Expansion (2026-07-23, at EPA):** 187 utilities/co-ops/developers + **23 governors (all Republican)**, incl. AEP, Dominion, Duke, Exelon, NextEra, PG&E, Southern, TVA, Xcel; developers Aligned, CyrusOne, Digital Realty, Equinix, QTS, STACK, Vantage; governors of AL, AK, AR, GA, ID, IN, IA, LA, MS, MO, MT, NE, NV, ND, OH, OK, SC, SD, TN, TX, UT, WV, WY. Claimed coverage "roughly 80% of the power delivered to American homes and businesses." Voluntary, nonregulatory. Zeldin: "The global race for AI is underway, and America must win it — but not by asking working families to pay for it". https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/president-trump-expands-historic-ratepayer-protection-pledge-protect-american ; https://www.powermag.com/white-house-expands-data-center-ratepayer-pledge-as-congress-moves-to-codify-protections/
