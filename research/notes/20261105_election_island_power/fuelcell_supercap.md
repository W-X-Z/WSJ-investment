## 1. Bloom Energy (SOFC) — why supercaps, what ships, numbers

### 1a. Why SOFC cannot follow step loads on its own
- Bloom whitepaper "Load Following Solid Oxide Fuel Cell" (Feb 2024), https://www.bloomenergy.com/wp-content/uploads/load-following-solid-oxide-fuel-cell.pdf
  - p.8: tests describe the worst-case step with an "extreme-case slope of 2000%/min" (i.e., ~33%/s — [INFERENCE] unit conversion). Fuel cells are "paired with an inverter and supercapacitors"; Fig. 8 shows step load between 40% and 100% "using supercapacitors for ramp support"; after a load drop "the fuel cell ramps down to 40% of capacity almost immediately". Supercap banks supply power instantly during steps, then are recharged by the fuel cell after it reaches target power. No %/s ramp rate for the stack itself, no ms response figure, no kWh sizing is stated. Batteries are not mentioned.
  - p.7: an "AI load simulator" (controllable resistor) mimics near-instantaneous step loads; output power quality meets ITIC, IEC 62040, SEMI.
  - p.9 Table 1: Bloom efficiency 0.54 at full load, 0.53 at 30% load vs microturbine 0.3716 / 0.257.
- Bloom "Be Flexible" launch PR, 2024-02-12, https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240212151740/en : "follow an electricity load increase from 40 to 100 percent almost instantaneously"; "more than 5 times faster power ramp than legacy solutions for load following"; Peter Gross: "Data center power demands can nowadays easily jump from 50 percent to 100 percent in a matter of seconds." No supercap mention. DCD coverage: https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/bloom-energy-adds-variable-load-ability-to-its-fuel-cells
- Bloom blog "The AI revolution: how fuel cells are solving the data center power challenge" (2025-10-08, updated 2026-09-22), https://www.bloomenergy.com/blog/the-ai-revolution-how-fuel-cells-are-solving-the-data-center-power-challenge/ : "A single server's power load might fluctuate from 15kW to 30kW within seconds", "scaling up to 50-100 MW swings at the data center level"; "batteries have a limited number of charge-discharge cycles"; "with the addition of supecapacitors [sic], fuel cells are very well suited to handle 'spiky' power loads"; fuel cells "don't manage rapidly fluctuating power loads through frequent startups and shutdowns... they're a primary-power technology meant to run continuously on a 24/7 baseload." FAQ (paraphrase): Bloom responds to load increases at least 2x faster than rotating generators, steps down instantly; paired with supercaps the system reaches 100% response in milliseconds; fuel cell + supercap combination achieves 200,000+ charge cycles with 99.9% availability.
- Independent technical anchor (Microsoft/CMU, HPCA 2016) "Techniques for Efficiently Handling Power Surges in Fuel Cell Powered Data Centers", https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.04506 : a 12.5 kW fuel cell system has a "load following rate of 16 W/s" (≈0.13%/s [INFERENCE]); a 1 kW/s surge caused a large shortfall; "we assume that this ESD is a supercapacitor"; "it is the ramp rate that requires capping, rather than the peak power"; example sizing "ESD with a minimum capacity of 91 kJ" for a 4.7 kW surge (≈19 s of surge energy [INFERENCE]); supercap cost "approximately U.S. $20,000 per kWh"; supercap lifetime >20 years in all cases. (Older PEM/PAFC-era figures; Bloom's SOFC with hot-standby ramps faster, but the structural point—ramp limit, not peak—is the same.)

## 3. Are supercaps required, or can Li-ion BESS do it? Peer approaches

- Magnitude/speed of the problem (SemiAnalysis 2025-06-25, https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/ai-training-load-fluctuations-at-gigawatt-scale-risk-of-power-grid-blackout ): load swings "from full load to nearly idle in fractions of a second"; Meta LLaMa-3: swings "on the order of tens of megawatts" on a 24k-H100 (~30 MW) cluster; Google chart ~15 MW vs 1.5 MW fluctuation AI vs cloud; fossil generator ramp "5–50 MW/min"; "pytorch_no_powerplant_blowup=1" dummy-load flag costs tens of $M/yr at GW scale. Tesla's pitch: BESS discharges "hundreds of megawatts within seconds", handles ms/s/min scales, and (Tesla claim) exceeds capacitor banks which "cannot smooth load at the second scale"; 100 MW/2h BESS $38–80M. Synchronous condensers ~$30–60k/MVAr (~$10–20M per GW). Fuel cell/supercap section paywalled.
- Why supercaps for sub-second, high-frequency swings (sources): Bloom blog — batteries "have a limited number of charge-discharge cycles" vs "200,000+ charge cycles" for supercap+fuel cell (paraphrase); Microsoft/CMU paper — supercap lifetime >20 yrs under constant surge duty, ~$20k/kWh; DCK 2026-09-28 (https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/energy-power-supply/can-supercapacitors-smooth-ai-s-power-swings- ): supercaps charge/discharge "on a millisecond-to-second timescale", higher power density/lower energy density than batteries, EDLC ride-through ~2 min, hybrids up to 10 min; Flex's Chris Butler: "Instantaneous load transients can reach more than 150% of a power shelf's rated capacity during certain AI compute events." [INFERENCE] For a 10–20 MW swing every ~54 s (Bloom test profile 45s@90%/9s@10%), a Li-ion BESS would see ~580k cycles/yr at high C-rate — far outside Li-ion cycle life (thousands–tens of thousands); supercaps (≥500k–1M cycles, seconds of energy) fit, batteries are the wrong tool unless heavily oversized. Nothing found says BESS *cannot* do it technically; the issue is cycle life/cost at that duty.
- Peer approaches:
  - xAI Colossus (Memphis): Tesla Megapacks as buffer alongside gas turbines; 150 MW grid draw, turbines for initial 4 months (SemiAnalysis 2025-06-25). Colossus 2 reported ~3.3 GWh Megapacks (basenor blog, unverified).
  - VoltaGrid/Oracle Texas 2.3 GW (2025-10-15, POWER https://www.powermag.com/oracle-taps-voltagrid-for-2-3-gw-modular-gas-fleet-to-power-ai-data-centers-across-texas/ ): INNIO Jenbacher recips + ABB; "zero reliance on battery storage"; goal to "dampen swings in AI power demand". ABB supplies 35 synchronous condensers with flywheels (2026-03-25, https://www.abb.com/global/en/news/134418 ) for "instantaneous inertia", "transient performance requirements". No supercaps.
  - Microsoft: no on-site generation path confirmed in retrieved sources (its 2016 research assumed supercap UPS with fuel cells). Crusoe (Stargate Abilene): GE Vernova turbines on-site (DCD) — storage details not retrieved.
  - Nvidia GB300 NVL72 (2025-07-28, https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/how-new-gb300-nvl72-features-provide-steady-power-for-ai/ ): rack-level smoothing with "electrolytic capacitors" (65 J/GPU, ~half the PSU volume), power cap ramp-up, hardware "power burner" ramp-down; 30% lower peak grid demand; "The energy storage mechanism is only used to optimize the load profile".
  - AEP Wyoming/Google and Nebius/CoreWeave Bloom sites: no storage architecture disclosed.
- Scale precedent: no operating data center with GW-scale supercaps found. Largest cited: Skeleton ">100 MW of systems already deployed across the US" (all applications, DCD Feb 2026); Bloom AI-infra fleet ~250 MW across ~two dozen customers (Aug 2026); MiTAC Fremont islanded microgrid (MW undisclosed). The "58 MW precedent" in the task brief could not be sourced — open question.
