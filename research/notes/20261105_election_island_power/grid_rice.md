## Q1. Large-load interconnection policy

### 1.1 GRID Savings Act of 2026 (Heinrich) — what the bill text says

Primary: bill text PDF on Senate ENR Committee site — https://www.energy.senate.gov/services/files/989662CC-3B2C-4C7D-962F-793E028EC7AB ; section-by-section — https://www.energy.senate.gov/services/files/94985978-3994-4CCE-85C2-50E7D33516F2 ; one-pager — https://www.energy.senate.gov/services/files/7A1B5274-0079-434D-9679-798F76408D82 ; press release 2026-08-03 — https://www.energy.senate.gov/2026/8/heinrich-introduces-legislation-to-ensure-large-users-of-electricity-pay-for-grid-upgrades-protect-customers-from-costs-of-data-center-expansion

- Short title (Sec. 1): "Guarding Ratepayers from Increased Demand-costs Savings Act of 2026" = "GRID Savings Act of 2026". Introduced Monday 2026-08-03 (ABQ Journal 2026-08-04). **Bill number not located on congress.gov via search** — open item (see end). Note: S.3852 (Hawley, 2026-02-11) is a different "GRID Act" (Guaranteeing Rate Insulation from Data Centers), 20 MW threshold, "Zero Rate Effect Certificate" (BillTrack50).
- Sec. 2 adds new Federal Power Act §224 "Large load interconnection":
  - §224(a)(3) covered large load: "aggregate requested or projected peak demand of not less than 150 megawatts behind a single point of interconnection"; new or expanded, nonresidential; common ownership/control/affiliation at one site or contiguous/adjacent sites incl. phased development. §224(a)(3)(B): coverage applies whether or not the load is studied/served with generation or storage, and peak demand is **not reduced** by co-located resources.
  - §224(a)(5) "dedicated resource": may be "behind the meter of, co-located with, or proximate to" the load if electrically deliverable; must be new/incremental generation, storage, or enforceable flexibility under metered commitments.
  - §224(b) FERC jurisdiction over interconnection of covered large loads, incl. flexible interconnection service (subject to enforceable operating limits), provisional/phased service, and (b)(3)(C) studying and interconnecting a load together with associated generation/storage/verifiable flexibility.
  - §224(c)(1) rulemaking: final rule "not later than 1 year after the date of enactment"; applies to each transmitting utility; pro forma provisions plus regionally tailored requirements. (c)(3) FERC sets criteria for qualifying loads.
  - §224(d) required standards: (1) study process — timelines, readiness requirements, procedures protecting other customers from speculative/duplicative requests; must present alternatives (flexible/phased service, advanced transmission technologies) with cost and schedule and let customer choose; (2) facility classification — Interconnection Facilities, Direct Assignment Facilities, Network Upgrades, Stand Alone Network Upgrades; (3) cost allocation — Interconnection/Direct Assignment Facilities 100% assigned to customer; Network Upgrades allocated to the large-load customer and **credited back against transmission service charges** under standard (generator-interconnection-style) procedures; option to build; (4) payment and financial security protecting customers if load does not proceed; transparency to customers, state commissions, FERC; (5) reliability consistency with FPA §215 standards.
  - §224(e) voluntary funding of regional/interregional facilities selected in an accepted planning process, in exchange for "a Commission-defined right to transmission service enabled by that facility." Section-by-section quote: "Any voluntary funding shall not impact any regional or interregional cost allocation, save for reducing costs."
  - §224(f)(2) excludes ERCOT (transmission wholly within ERCOT). No express state savings clause beyond (f)(1) and state-commission disclosure rights in (d)(4).
- One-pager framing: applies to "large electricity customers (150MW or higher)" "the same basic approach" power plants face (detailed review, pay for connection upgrades, financial commitments).

**Does it make interconnection faster or just more expensive?** Text requires FERC to set study *timelines* and readiness screens (speed/queue hygiene) and makes the large load pay up-front for network upgrades *with crediting* (i.e., participant funding with reimbursement — generator-style), plus an optional "fund backbone transmission, get service rights" lane. The bill contains **no numeric study deadline** (e.g., 60/90 days) — only a 1-year FERC rulemaking deadline. Latitude Media (2026-08-04, secondary) characterizes it as creating "a path to faster interconnection in exchange for funding those upgrades." **[INFERENCE]** Because peak demand is explicitly *not* netted for co-located generation (§224(a)(3)(B)) and dedicated BTM resources are folded into FERC-jurisdictional study, the bill does not carve out a lighter path for behind-the-meter configurations; it treats them inside the same interconnection framework. Net incentive effect on BTM generation is ambiguous in the text itself.

### 1.3 FERC 2026 — DOE ANOPR (RM26-4-000) → six FPA §206 show-cause orders (no final rule)

- Oct 2025: DOE Sec. Wright directed FERC under DOE Org. Act §403 to issue an ANOPR on large-load interconnection; docket RM26-4-000; DOE asked for final action by 2026-04-30; >230 comments (McGuireWoods 2026-02, secondary). ANOPR had proposed a 20 MW threshold (Bracewell 2026-06-25, secondary).
- 2026-04-16: FERC "Order Regarding Intent to Act" (195 FERC ¶ 61,045) — will act by June 2026. FERC news: https://ferc.gov/news-events/news/ferc-act-large-load-interconnection-docket-june-2026 . Swett: "Our nation stands at a pivotal moment as we face rapid growth in demand from data centers."
- **2026-06-18**: FERC (5–0) issued six §206 show-cause orders to PJM, MISO, SPP, CAISO, ISO-NE, NYISO (PJM = 195 FERC ¶ 61,211; PJM docket EL26-67). Not a final rule; FERC said the show-cause route "should allow reforms to be implemented more quickly while accounting for regional differences" and left RM26-4 open for comments (Bracewell, secondary). Swett: "We are setting the stage for a resilient, reliable, and forward-thinking grid" (T&D World 2026-06-18, secondary).
  - Proposed large-load definition: new C&I customer at a single site, "peak load of 50 MW or greater," interconnecting above 69 kV, not part of a co-location arrangement (V&E; Bracewell; FERC Sept order quoting the show-cause: "appears that it would be reasonable to define a large load as…").
  - Five reform areas: (1) application/study process — rolling (non-cluster) intake, fees/deposits, site control/financial readiness, initial study results contemplated in **60–90 days**, pro forma service agreement, metering/telemetry/load management (V&E, secondary); (2) cost transparency and a pro forma **Cost Recovery Agreement** (minimum customer contribution toward directly attributable network upgrades, credit support — LOC/surety/cash); (3) co-location & BTM generation (not PJM, handled in EL25-49); (4) new services for **flexible large loads** "willing and able to limit their use"; (5) **electrically proximate large load** (within two substations of a generator POI) — options: dedicated study with interim service, modified ERIS/NRIS, or new load-limited interconnection service.
  - Deadlines: responses 60 days (≈2026-08-17); resource-adequacy informational report 30 days; abeyance requests 45 days. States keep retail service/siting/IRP/retail rate design (V&E). Non-RTO regions (Southeast, Northwest, desert SW) and ERCOT not covered; FERC "encourage[d]" non-RTO filings (Bracewell).
  - **PJM status (primary, FERC order 2026-09-21, EL26-67-001, 196 FERC ¶ 61,214, https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/documents/ferc/orders/2026/20260921-el26-67-001.pdf ):** rehearing/clarification requests dismissed because the show-cause order "did not make any final decision." An 2026-08-14 abeyance order moved PJM/TO responses to **2026-11-16** (answers 2026-12-16); if PJM/TOs file an FPA §205 proposal by 2026-11-16, the show-cause obligation is suspended. FERC "do[es] not intend to provide clarification or guidance" during abeyance.
- **[INFERENCE]** As of 2026-11-05 there is no FERC final rule on large-load interconnection; the operative federal mechanism is RTO-specific §206 proceedings with a PJM filing due 2026-11-16.

### 1.4 PJM co-location (EL25-49)

- 2025-12-18 order (193 FERC ¶ 61,217): PJM tariff "unjust and unreasonable" for lacking "clarity or consistency" on co-located load; PJM directed to create three new services beside NITS — Interim Non-Firm Transmission Service (for NITS-seekers), Firm Contract Demand, Non-Firm Contract Demand; paper hearing on rates/terms; revise BTMG rules + transition; co-located load that causes/benefits from network upgrades, black start or regulation pays for them. Compliance: 30 days (2026-01-19) and 60 days (2026-02-16). (Day Pitney, secondary.) PJM compliance filings 2026-01-20 and 2026-02-24; paper-hearing briefs Feb–Apr 2026 (McGuireWoods, secondary).
- **2026-06-18 order on rehearing/compliance/paper hearing (EL25-49-002)**: FERC news summary — modifies and partly sets aside the December order; establishes rates/terms/conditions for the new services; PJM and PJM TOs each must file further compliance "within 60 days." https://ferc.gov/news-events/news/summaries-june-2026-commission-meeting ; order PDF https://www.ferc.gov/sites/default/files/2026-06/EL25-49-002_0.pdf (not parsed). Troutman (secondary): set aside the requirement that BTMG-served Eligible Customers take NITS with no alternative; BTMG netting rules still unjust/unreasonable; materiality threshold to be load-based rather than generator-nameplate-based; add QF cogeneration exemption process; extend new services to BTMG loads. Brattle (2026-07, secondary): FERC ordered NFCDS broadly available (PJM wanted outage-only); curtailment gross-zero only where generator is a capacity resource, otherwise **net-zero**; PJM's 2029 implementation asked to be reconsidered; disconnection after a second (not first) curtailment failure; "grid reliance" charge deferred (Comm'r Chang concurrence flags cost-shift risk).
- **[INFERENCE]** Net-zero curtailment for co-located capacity resources plus broad NFCDS access increases the usable value of on-site generation in PJM relative to PJM's original proposal; the gross-zero rule for non-capacity generators cuts the other way.

### 1.5 ERCOT / PUCT SB 6 implementation

- SB 6 signed 2025-06-20, effective immediately; large load = **75 MW** or greater (White & Case 2026-08-05, secondary, fn.2). PUCT projects: 58479 (net metering with existing BTM gen), 58480 (forecasting), 58481 (interconnection standards), 58482 (reliability/demand reductions), 58484 (transmission cost allocation) (McGuireWoods, secondary).
- ERCOT primary (2026-06-01, https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/06/01/ERCOT-Trending-Topic-Grid-Reliability-Data-Centers-Net-Metering-Arrangements.pdf ): SB 6 review applies to ≥75 MW loads co-locating with generators connected before 2025-09-01; ERCOT technical review then PUCT approve/deny/condition. Approved: Apr 2026 — 265 MW data center + 265.5 MW wind (Armstrong County); May 2026 — 760 MW data center + 1,099 MW gas plant (Freestone County); three pending. Conditions: data center "must reduce use or switch to backup generation, and the plant must restore full output within 30 minutes of an ERCOT instruction"; ERCOT notice sequence Preparedness Notice → Action Notice (public) → Resume Operations; ERCOT says these are "for operational awareness and do not signal a grid emergency."
- PUCT Docket 59220 (2026-07-24, Goodnight Wind/Crusoe Load Two) per White & Case: co-located large loads must "fully curtail power consumption within 30 minutes of an ERCOT emergency instruction without financial compensation"; must rely on own output during grid emergencies (no "bring your own capacity"); barred from paid DR/ancillary/ERS/TDSP load-management; liable if a shared transmission asset carries ≥25% less power than expected; SB 6 process: notice to ERCOT → 120-day system impact study → PUCT decision within 60 days; review at 36–60 months. Settlement netting cites ERCOT Nodal Protocols §10.3.2.3(2)(a). Gov. Abbott letter 2026-08-03 directed ERCOT "Batch Zero" audit.

## Q2. Reciprocating gas engines (RICE) as prime power at data centers — CAA framework and actual permits

### 2.1 Federal rules (as applied in the permits below)

- **40 CFR 60 Subpart JJJJ** (NSPS, SI engines). As reproduced in Georgia Permit 4911-217-0080-S-01-0 Table 2.5-1 (citing 40 CFR 60.4233(e), 60.4234, Table 1): non-emergency SI natural gas (and lean-burn LPG) engines ≥500 HP (except lean burn 500≤HP<1,350) manufactured after 2010-07-01: **NOx 1.0 / CO 2.0 / VOC 0.7 g/HP-hr, or 82 / 270 / 60 ppmvd at 15% O2**. Earlier-vintage/landfill-digester tier shown as 2.0/4.0/1.0 g/HP-hr. (The Ohio EdgeConneX application cites JJJJ non-emergency limits as "NOx 2.0, CO 4.0, VOC 1.0 g/hp-hr" — that is the pre-2010 tier; the current ≥500 hp tier is 1.0/2.0/0.7.) Testing: initial performance test; for >500 hp, subsequent tests "every 8,760 hours or 3 years" (GA permit Cond. 6.x). Emergency engines: 100 hr/yr maintenance/testing incl. ≤50 hr non-emergency; no peak shaving/DR/financial arrangements (GA permit Cond. 2.6; PA AQTAC deck citing 40 CFR 63.6640(f)(1)).
- **40 CFR 63 Subpart ZZZZ** (NESHAP RICE). New SI 4SLB ≥500 hp at a **major HAP source**: "CO reduction of 93% or more, or formaldehyde at or below 14 ppmvd at 15% O2; catalyst inlet temperature 450–1350 °F; startup not to exceed 30 minutes" (Ohio EdgeConneX New Albany North PTI application, Trinity Consultants, 2025-03). New engines at **area** HAP sources comply with ZZZZ by meeting JJJJ (GA permit Cond. 2.7; OK DEQ Citadel permit).
- **Aggregation ("single source")**: EPA three-factor test — same industrial grouping (SIC), contiguous/adjacent, common control (incl. 2018 "Meadowbrook" common-control memo). Practice observed:
  - Georgia EPD (VoltaGrid/Serverfarm): draft narrative 2025-12-02 said *separate* sources (different SIC; fenced parcels; Meadowbrook). Final (Addendum B, 2026): "Based on a contractual agreement EPD became aware of after the issuance of the draft permit, the Division has determined that VoltaGrid and ATL2 are a single, minor NSR site. VoltaGrid is considered a support facility for the data center and is under common control." Two permits, one shared 99.0 tpy NOx cap.
  - Ohio (EdgeConneX New Albany North): applicant treats power engines "as part of the same industrial grouping as the data center they support"; a separate EdgeConneX plant (Innovation Campus Way, Ohio EPA ID 0145000616) not aggregated because not contiguous/adjacent.
  - PA DEP (Amazon Falls Twp 09-0261A): future projects under common control with same SIC must be aggregated with Phases 1–2 for major-source/Title V/NSR.
  - Each engine is an emission unit (EU1…EU33 etc.) under one facility permit; facility-wide caps are what create minor/synthetic-minor status.
- EPA proposed rule 2026-05-13 (91 FR 26958 per SELC letter) redefining "begin actual construction" as physical on-site construction of "pollutant-emitting activities"; comments to 2026-06-29 (V&E, secondary). Generator pads, SCR housings, stacks likely count as emitting activities.

### 2.6 250 MW continuous: J624 + SCR vs aeroderivative + SCR @ 5 ppm (all **[INFERENCE]**, arithmetic from permit factors)
- J624: 4,360 kWe (60 Hz), 46.2% electrical efficiency (INNIO fact sheet via Imperial College); uncontrolled NOx "<1.1 g/bhp-hr" (500 mg/Nm³ @5% O2). 250 MW ≈ 57 engines.
  - At PA DEP BAT limit 0.05 g/hp-hr: ~6,290 bhp/engine [1.43 hp per ekW, from J620 4,287 hp/3,000 kW] × 57 × 8,760 h × 0.05 g ≈ 157 t ≈ **~173 short tpy**.
  - At permitted steady-state factors (0.18 lb/hr per ~3.0–3.3 MW J620 → ~0.055–0.06 lb/MWh): 250 MW × 8,760 × 0.06 ≈ **~66 tpy**; scaling VoltaGrid GA PTE (26.88 tpy/90 MW) → **~75 tpy**; scaling EdgeConneX OH Wärtsilä PTE (161.3 tpy/462 MW) → **~87 tpy**.
- Aeroderivative simple cycle with SCR at 5 ppmvd @15% O2: 1 ppm ≈ 0.00368 lb/MMBtu (Fd 8,710 dscf/MMBtu; ×20.9/5.9) → 5 ppm ≈ 0.0184 lb/MMBtu. At ~9.5 MMBtu/MWh HHV simple-cycle heat rate: 250 MW × 8,760 × 9.5 × 0.0184 ≈ 383,000 lb ≈ **~190 tpy**. At 2.5 ppm (Valara SC permit factors: 4.59 lb/hr on 453.4 MMBtu/hr ≈ 0.0101 lb/MMBtu ≈ 2.7 ppm) ≈ **~95 tpy**.
- Reading: recip fleets as actually permitted in GA/PA/WA/OH (0.14–0.18 lb/hr per ~3 MW J620; ~1.5 lb/hr per 19 MW W18V50SG) sit at roughly **0.25–0.35 tpy NOx per MW-yr**, i.e., near or below a 5-ppm turbine (~0.76 tpy/MW-yr) and comparable to a ~2–3 ppm turbine. The gap narrows if the recip fleet is held only to the 0.05 g/hp-hr BAT ceiling (~0.7 tpy/MW-yr). Recips carry larger CO/VOC/formaldehyde and NH3 burdens (EdgeConneX PTE: CO 219, VOC 244, NH3 214 tpy) that drive Title V/HAP-major status even when NOx is minor.
