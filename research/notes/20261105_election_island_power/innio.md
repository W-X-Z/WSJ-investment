## 3. Backlog definition, prepayments, cancellation terms, timing

### Definitions (8-K Ex.99.1 "Key Performance Indicators", 2026-10-27; same in 424B4)
> "Equipment Order Intake is defined as the booking of a new sales order for the Equipment segment within a given period when specific criteria are met, including a signed contract, defined scope, fixed price, delivery schedule, and fully defined terms and conditions. The order must have a low probability of cancellation, all necessary approvals and risk reviews completed, and any required down payment (if any) received."
> "Equipment Order Backlog is defined as Equipment Order Intake that has not yet been fulfilled towards the customer."
424B4 footnote: "Normal book-to-bill cycle for the Equipment segment is 3-12 months; however, this can range up to three years, depending on how far ahead customers place orders and/or the availability of production slots".

**Slot reservations are NOT in backlog.** Q3 deck glossary: "Slot Reservation: Defined as a contractual agreement between a customer and INNIO reserving a dedicated production slot for Equipment." Q2 deck (as of 2026-06-30): "Combined Equipment Order Backlog and Slot Reservations of >15 GW"; "~64% of >15 GW related to BTM data center solutions"; "Within Data Center, ~94% related to prime power". Q3 deck slide 24: installed fleet ~44 GW + "Visible growth ~19 GW¹ Backlog & reserved slots" (as of Q3'26). Call (Schulze): reservations "will turn into order between 6 months from now and 18 months from now"; "we have a line of sight up to 2029, 2030, 2031"; "We still have capacity available for 29 and 30 as capacity will be released"; "We will release additional slots in measured, conservative stages." Q3 deck slide 14: "Non-cancellable, non-tradeable slots for high-quality projects".
**INFERENCE:** ~19 GW combined vs $8.0B backlog; at the Q3 implied ~$0.65M/MW equipment price (777.6M / 1.2 GW) the $8.0B backlog ≈ 12 GW, so slot reservations ≈ 7 GW on top — not firm orders.

### Cancellation / financing protection
- Call (CFO Schulze, 2026-10-27): "most equipment orders are take or pay. With associated down payments and cancellation fees." (No fee %, no refund terms disclosed.)
- 424B4 Note 1.17: "For large equipment orders, the Company may issue progress billings, primarily to secure production slots. In general, all of the Company's billings are due and paid within 30 to 180 days of issuance." … "The Company may receive advance payments from customers, including in some cases, more than 12 months before delivery. These advance payments are typically required to reserve production capacity, secure long-lead materials…"
- Revenue recognition is point-in-time at title transfer for "a substantial majority" of equipment (424B4 Note 1.17).
