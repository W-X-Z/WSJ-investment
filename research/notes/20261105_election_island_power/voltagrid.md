## 1. Ownership and capital

**Founding / history**
- Founded 2020, Houston. CEO/founder Nathan Ough (ex-Macquarie banker, co-founder of Certarus CNG). Originally on-site gas generation for oil & gas (electric frac fleets with Halliburton/Diamondback in the Permian, Dec 2024). (Bloomberg via EnergyConnects 2026-02-24 https://www.energyconnects.com/news/gas-lng/2026/february/voltagrid-weighing-public-listing-or-sale-riding-the-ai-wave ; WorldOil 2024-12-16)
- 2021 round US$73M incl. **CPP Investments** (The Logic https://thelogic.co/briefing/cpp-backed-data-centre-firm-voltagrid-raises-us5b-in-debt/). GEM wiki lists owners of the Georgia entity as CPP Investment Board 33.3% / Longbow Capital 33.3% / natural persons 33.3% (secondary wiki, pre-Blackstone; treat as indicative). PitchBook total raised US$7.4B (The Logic, Nov 2025 — likely includes the $5B debt) [Inference].

**Debt (2025-11-10)** — $2.0B second-lien notes due 2030 (Goldman left lead; JPM, BMO, TD, Wells, MUFG, NBC, Scotia, Barclays, CIBC, Citi, Mizuho, MS, Texas Capital) + $3.0B ABL (JPMorgan Chase admin agent). Use: "funding growth initiatives, refinancing existing debt, and supporting long-term investment in distributed power generation assets." CFO Micah Foster: "VoltaGrid has had a transformational year of growth in our data center business." Coupon not in release. Fitch (per Bloomberg) estimates EBITDA ≈ **$1.1B by 2028**, >5x 2024 level.

**Equity (2026)** — 2026-05-11 announcement: $1.0B = $775M primary + $225M secondary; acquisition of **Propell Energy Technology Ltd.** (founded 1978, ~1,000 staff US/Canada, manufactures VoltaGrid's QPac; Granbury TX plants; target **~300 MW/month** manufacturing across recips and turbines). Statement: deal "expected to materially reduce execution risk" across the order book. Closing announced 2026-10-05 (primary $775M done; secondary upsized to $800M, "no proceeds to the company"; placement agents JPM, Barclays, TD). Propell closing not confirmed in the Oct-5 release — **open question**.
- Feb 2026 Bloomberg: company weighed IPO or sale; Blackstone and BlackRock in talks; >$10B valuation.

**Oracle relationship** — Oracle is a **customer/collaboration partner**, not a disclosed investor. 2025-10-15: "strategic collaboration" to deploy "more than 2,300 MW" for OCI AI data centers in Texas, with Energy Transfer firm gas, INNIO Jenbacher + ABB; >750 jobs; Dallas/Houston manufacturing expansion (Sidley https://www.sidley.com/en/newslanding/newsannouncements/2025/10/sidley-represents-voltagrid-in-its-collaboration-with-oracle ; PGJ 2025-10-16 https://pgjonline.com/news/2025/october/energy-transfer-to-supply-voltagrid-powering-oracle-s-23-gw-ai-data-centers). Oracle EVP Mahesh Thiagarajan: "AI workloads are uniquely power-intensive and highly variable, often creating swings in demand." No source found for an Oracle equity investment in VoltaGrid. Oracle FY2025 10-K does not name VoltaGrid (Telborg briefing).

**Chevron** — no VoltaGrid–Chevron link found. Chevron's data-center power play is with Engine No. 1 / GE Vernova (see §7). **Halliburton** is the strategic (Oct 2025 global data-center distributed power MoU, Middle East first; Dec 2025 Halliburton secured 400 MW of manufacturing capacity for eastern-hemisphere data centers — DCD 2026-05).

## 4. Permits

**Texas (Shackelford / ABI-1)**
- Reg. **180842** (EGU Standard Permit, minor source): 210 engines / 709 MW; TCEQ signed 2025-07-17 → **issued/approved** (measuredai: "TCEQ-approved permit to operate 210 Jenbacher"). Title V application to follow.
- **Permit 182467 + PSDTX1692 + GHGPSDTX266** (major NSR/PSD, expedited review): applied 2025-12-12, NORI 2025-12-17; J620/J624 with SCR + oxidation catalysts; proposed emissions NOx 569.9 tpy, CO 917.6, VOC 523.1, PM 378.3, SO2 196.8, **CO2e 10.63 Mtpy**. TCEQ mailed **Notice of Application and Preliminary Decision 2026-09-22 — one day after Gov. Abbott's 2026-09-21 halt order**; newspaper publication 2026-09-28 (public hearing notice dated 2026-09-23). Public Citizen asked commissioners to withdraw (2026-10-07); TCEQ had not. **Status: pending; in public comment / contested-case window; final issuance subject to the pause.** (TCEQ PLS https://www.tceq.texas.gov/downloads/permitting/air/bilingual/pending-permit-notices/182467-pls-english.pdf ; Public Citizen https://www.citizen.org/wp-content/uploads/Summary_-Data-Center-Permits-Issued-by-TCEQ-After-9_21_2026.pdf ; KUT 2026-10-20 https://www.kut.org/energy-environment/2026-10-20/texas-continued-permitting-data-centers-after-abbotts-pause-report-finds)
- **Pause mechanics** (Bracewell): Abbott letter 2026-09-21 to "halt all permits sought by data centers"; applies to affiliates building generation for a data center and to fully islanded BTM sites; until ERCOT audit completes (~mid-Dec 2026); already-issued permits not affected; TCEQ on 2026-10-19 said it is "screening pending permit applications" and pausing those subject to the directive; Governor's office: "No permits have been approved." Earlier orders 2026-08-03 (PUCT/ERCOT audit) and 2026-09-14 (TWDB water survey; non-compliance = ineligible for TCEQ permits). (https://www.bracewell.com/resources/governor-abbott-orders-tceq-to-pause-all-permit-issuance-for-data-center-projects/)
- [Inference] Phase-1 709 MW is permitted; the step-up to ~2–2.58 GW (needed for the 1.4 GW IT target and the "64% overbuild") depends on 182467, which is caught in the pause.

**Non-Texas**: Georgia (Covington) — EPD violation notice, construction paused Jul-2026, permit pending. Washington (Quincy) — withdrawn Jul-2026. Other states: none found.

## 6. Business model economics

- Model: energy-as-a-service / "prime-as-a-service" under long-term PPAs; VoltaGrid procures, builds, owns, operates (SemiAnalysis). INNIO claims >$300M/yr fuel savings at 2.3 GW vs alternatives and up to 10 pp higher efficiency.
- **Price**: undisclosed. Rabobank (2026-06-24, revised 2026-10-22) unsubsidized model: recip engines ≈ **$107/MWh** at year 5 vs US industrial grid avg ≈ $86/MWh (EIA 2025); engines fall to ~$80 by yr 9 then reset ~$100 at overhaul; CCGT ≈ $55 at yr 15. Rabobank classifies **VoltaGrid as a "bridge" EaaS provider** using contracts "with built-in termination provisions"; bridge equipment "meant for 3–7 years" may end up as primary power. (https://www.rabobank.com/knowledge/d011532274-the-marathon-can-behind-the-meter-data-center-power-solutions-endure-the-decade-in-the-us)
- Bridge vs permanent at Frontier: companies describe gas as "a necessary bridge while renewable and storage technologies scale" (Business Insider via ConstructionOwners); Oracle lease ~15 yrs; Vantage keeps a grid connection option. VoltaGrid's engines are containerized/redeployable (INNIO S-1: "engineered for rapid deployment and redeployment across sites").
- **Stranded-asset risk**: [Inference] sits with VoltaGrid as asset owner (financed via ABL + 2nd-lien notes) mitigated by (a) take-or-pay/termination provisions in PPAs (terms undisclosed) and (b) asset mobility (25 MW modular packs). Rabobank: bridge = "manageable downside"; heavy-duty long-duration = "most exposed downside."
