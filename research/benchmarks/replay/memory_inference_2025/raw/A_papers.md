# Branch A — Academic papers (point-in-time scan, as of 2025-09-30)

- Replay date: **2025-09-30**. Only papers whose **v1 submission date is on or before 2025-09-30** are included. Every abstract was read from the version-pinned page `arxiv.org/abs/<id>v1`, so post-cutoff revisions were not seen.
- Coverage: 3 AI hardware supply chains: (1) memory / HBM / advanced packaging / storage, (2) optical interconnect, (3) data-center power and cooling.
- Rows: **45** (all title+abstract fetched). Another 8 papers were fetched but left out to keep the table to 45; they are listed in the appendix.

## Method note: deviation from the requested arXiv-API method

- **The arXiv API (`export.arxiv.org/api/query`) refused WebFetch** (`robots_disallowed`), and so did `arxiv.org/search/`. No curl, mirror or cache was used. These URLs are listed under **Denied**.
- **Discovery used other routes instead:**
  - (a) **31 WebSearch queries** limited to `arxiv.org`, spread across all 7 topic areas (list at the bottom).
  - (b) **Chronological cs.AR monthly listings** for 2025-08 and 2025-09. Only entries 1–50 of each month could be read, because the `skip` parameter was ignored.
- **Each kept paper was then fetched through WebFetch from its `arxiv.org/abs/<id>v1` page.**
- **Hindsight guards**
  - **Post-cutoff search results discarded unread.** Search results surfaced many papers with IDs 2510–2610; their abstracts were never opened and none of them informs this file.
  - **Search ranking is a residual bias.** Results are ranked by relevance and popularity computed *after* the cutoff. This may push already-popular papers up. The cs.AR chronological listings partly offset it: 7 of the 45 rows came from them.
  - **Dates from the unversioned page.** For 2506.04301, 2506.12160 and 2509.07218, the v1 page did not show a date. The `[v1]` line was read from the unversioned page's submission history, and only that metadata line was used.
  - **2509.22680 has no visible submission history.** Its date is the ID month (2025-09) plus the date printed in the document (2025-09-15).
- **"Quantitative claim" wording:** the numbers are as stated in the abstract. WebFetch relays abstracts as close paraphrases, because the tool caps verbatim quotes, so the wording is not verbatim.
- **"New term?" is an analyst judgment** using only knowledge from before the cutoff. New means the term or mechanism first appeared within about 12 months before 2025-09-30, i.e. roughly Oct 2024 or later.

## Recurrence note (terms in 3+ papers)

1. **KV-cache tiering below HBM** (CPU DRAM, SSD, CXL, SmartNIC-fetched remote KV, prefix/context caching) appears in 6 papers: 2504.11816, 2509.16857, 2508.18572, 2507.07400, 2509.03377, 2508.08457. This pulls on DRAM, CXL expanders, DPU/NIC and PCIe bandwidth. SSD as a weight tier is challenged on energy (2508.06978: ~12x per-token energy).
2. **The "memory wall"** (HBM bandwidth plus capacity) as the binding decode limit, now framed around agentic, long-context and reasoning workloads, appears in 7 papers: 2507.14397, 2509.09505, 2508.08457, 2509.03377, 2508.07252, 2506.04301, 2505.14733. Proposed remedies point to HBM4, 3D DRAM-on-logic, hybrid-bonded NMP (2509.09420) and large on-die memory.
3. **Counter-signal: KV compression and reuse** cut KV memory by 47–90% in 4 papers (2505.24133: 90%; 2506.18879: 87.5%; 2509.03377: 46.9%; 2507.21433: +40–60% throughput). Net HBM demand per token depends on whether context and output length grow faster than compression improves.
4. **Optical circuit switching and reconfigurable photonic fabrics** replacing electrical switch tiers appear in 7 papers: 2502.03885, 2507.08119, 2509.15450, 2508.03674, 2507.14000, 2508.06079, 2506.15006. Separately, **microring / coherent / DSP-free CPO at ~400G per λ** appears in 4: 2506.12160, 2509.01555, 2509.20584, 2505.18534. Together they imply more OCS, SiPh modulators and lasers/combs, and fewer high-radix electrical switches and DSPs.
5. **AI-training power swings driving grid oscillation, power stabilization and energy buffering** appear in 6 papers: 2508.14318, 2508.16457, 2509.22680 (±400 Vdc, SST), 2502.01647, 2507.00909, 2509.07218. These favour power electronics and fast storage. Thermal limits of 3D stacks recur in 3 more (2509.00633, 2508.07252, 2507.18040), next to the liquid and two-phase cooling rows.
